Question

“Nigeria is getting worse” is one number, and nothing in it says where. Which states got worse, and which months were deadliest?

Claim

The headline is true. 29,300 events and 94,586 deaths, 2015 to 2025 (data). A national rate of +22.9 per cent a year (model).

It is also the least useful number in the file.

Counter-claim

Two facts a national average cannot carry.

The state with the most deaths is not the state worsening fastest (model). And a deadlier month is not a busier one (data).

Setup

One row per state, LGA, year and month: 772 cells, 37 states, 2015 to 2026 (data). The analysis fits 2015 to 2025. 2026 is six months and still being revised, so it sits out.

Every number is tagged: (data) counted in the file, (model) fitted, (proof) arithmetic.

The analysis

Thirty-seven speeds, one direction

The national rate is the middle of a wide spread. State trends run from +1.5 to +59.8 per cent a year (model), a standard deviation of 14.5 points (model). Thirty-three states are worse than flat. Four are flat, and none improves (model).

Sokoto leads at +59.8 per cent (model). Its toll in eleven years is 2,372 deaths (data). Borno’s is 35,355 (data).

Pattern: every interval but four sits right of zero; the spread is nearly the size of the national trend itself.

The deadliest state is not the fastest

Borno is +8.9 per cent a year (model), the third slowest in the country. It carries 30 per cent of all deaths since 2019 (data).

Rank the states by toll and by speed, and the lists barely overlap: rank correlation 0.21 (model). The fastest risers are small: Kwara has 430 deaths in eleven years (data), one-eightieth of Borno’s.

Pattern: Borno sits far right and low; the high points sit left. Bigger is not faster.

Ten of the twelve deadliest months are Borno

The top twelve state-months by deaths, 2019 to 2025, are overwhelmingly one state: ten are Borno, and they hold 84 per cent of those deaths (data).

The exception is the point of the chart. Niger, March 2022: 491 deaths from 28 events (data), 17.5 per event, against Borno’s 5 to 12. One LGA, Mariga, took 302 of them in five events (data).

Across states, events and deaths rank together at 0.86 (data). Events and deaths per event rank at 0.39 (data). Busy and lethal are not the same list.

Pattern: one colour fills ten rows. The odd row is not a big month; it is a lethal one.

Deaths have a season; events do not

Events by calendar month are flat: 288 to 320 a month, on average, across equal years (data). Deaths are not. January to June average 891 deaths a month-year; July to October, 700 (data). Ratio 1.27, on the same years.

Both series are indexed to their own mean, so the shapes compare directly (proof).

Pattern: the bars bow upward in the first half; the line does not move.

NoteWhere the numbers come from

The file. Kaggle justtryit01/nigerian-insecurity-analysis-1997-2026, MIT licence, compiled from ACLED (1997–2026) and UCDP (1990–2024) via HDX. The version here is acled_cleaned.csv, filtered to 2015–2026 and committed as data/acled_clean_lga_month.csv: 106,536 rows, balanced, no duplicates, no negatives. data/PROVENANCE.md records the source and the filter. 84.8 per cent of these LGA-months record no events, which is why the trend model is a count model rather than a line through totals.

The model. One negative binomial with a state-varying slope, pv_events ~ t + (1 + t | state) on the state-month panel, 37 × 132 rows, fitted with glmmTMB. The state estimate is the country slope plus that state’s conditional mode; the interval is the conditional standard deviation, so a small state’s trend is pulled toward the national one and a large state’s is left alone. The national rate is the fixed effect. analysis.R reproduces every number here and caches the one fit that takes time; delete cache/ to refit.

“Worse”, three ways. A state is worse when its interval excludes zero: 33 states. Requiring +10 per cent a year as well: 31. Applying Benjamini–Hochberg across the 37: 33. The post reports the first; the other two are in analysis.R, because the list shrinking under a stricter rule is a fact about the rule, not about Nigeria.

The deadliest months. Ranked on 2019–2025. 2026 is excluded deliberately: it holds six months, its June is thin (131 events against May’s 643), and ACLED revises recent months, so a 2026 month would win or lose this table on reporting lag rather than on deaths.

Caveats that belong here, not in a footnote. ACLED coverage deepens over time, so 2015 and 2025 are not the same reporting regime. Fatality counts in mass-casualty banditry are less reliable than event counts. There is no population column, so “worse” is per area, not per capita. The file carries no actors, event types, coordinates or exact dates, so “banditry against Borno” is not answerable from it.

What this post does not do. No forecast, no held-out score, no model comparison. The specification for that work is nigeria-conflict-methods-spec.md; this post is the descriptive pass that comes before it.

So the country is getting worse.

It is, and the average is the part that misleads. Borno carries one death in three and has barely moved since 2015. The fastest risers are small states with a fraction of its toll. Rank by speed and you get the north-west. Rank by deaths and you get Borno. Both lists are the country.

## The analysis

### Thirty-seven speeds, one direction

The national rate is the middle of a wide spread. State trends run from +1.5 to +59.8 per cent a year (model), a standard deviation of 14.5 points (model). Thirty-three states are worse than flat. Four are flat, and none improves (model).

Sokoto leads at +59.8 per cent (model). Its toll in eleven years is 2,372 deaths (data). Borno's is 35,355 (data).

```{r}
#| label: fig-trend
#| echo: false
#| fig-cap: "Thirty-seven speeds. Borno, the deadliest state, is fifth from the bottom."

tr <- trend[order(trend$pct), ]
tr$state <- factor(tr$state, levels = tr$state)
tr$col   <- ifelse(tr$worse, pal[["worse"]], pal[["flat"]])

fig1 <- plot_ly(data = tr, x = ~pct, y = ~state, height = 640,
  type = "scatter", mode = "markers",
  marker = list(color = ~col, size = 7),
  error_x = list(type = "data", symmetric = FALSE,
    array = ~(high - pct), arrayminus = ~(pct - low),
    color = "#9aa4b2", thickness = 1.1, width = 0),
  hovertemplate = "%{y}: %{x:.1f}% a year<extra></extra>")

fig1 <- layout(fig1,
  margin = list(l = 10, r = 60, t = 30, b = 10),
  showlegend = FALSE,
  shapes = list(list(type = "line", yref = "paper", y0 = 0, y1 = 1,
    x0 = national_pct, x1 = national_pct,
    line = list(color = pal[["ink"]], width = 1, dash = "dot"))),
  annotations = list(
    list(x = national_pct, yref = "paper", y = 1.0, xanchor = "center",
      yanchor = "bottom", showarrow = FALSE,
      text = sprintf("country: +%.1f", national_pct),
      font = list(size = 12, color = pal[["ink"]])),
    list(x = borno$pct, y = "Borno", xanchor = "left", yanchor = "bottom",
      showarrow = FALSE, xshift = 8,
      text = "Borno", font = list(size = 12, color = pal[["deaths"]])),
    list(x = trend$pct[trend$state == "Sokoto"], y = "Sokoto",
      xanchor = "right", yanchor = "bottom", showarrow = FALSE, xshift = -8,
      text = "Sokoto", font = list(size = 12, color = pal[["deaths"]]))),
  xaxis = list(title = "events per year, 2015-2025 (% change a year)",
    gridcolor = grid, zeroline = FALSE, tickfont = list(size = 12)),
  yaxis = list(title = "", tickfont = list(size = 11), gridcolor = grid))

config(fig1, displayModeBar = FALSE)
```

### The deadliest state is not the fastest

Borno is +8.9 per cent a year (model), the third slowest in the country. It carries 30 per cent of all deaths since 2019 (data).

Rank the states by toll and by speed, and the lists barely overlap: rank correlation 0.21 (model). The fastest risers are small: Kwara has 430 deaths in eleven years (data), one-eightieth of Borno's.

```{r}
#| label: fig-speed-size
#| echo: false
#| fig-cap: "Speed against size. The biggest risers are not the biggest tolls."

sc <- trend

                 ifelse(sc$worse, pal[["worse"]], pal[["flat"]]))
sc$show <- sc$state %in% c("Borno", "Sokoto", "Zamfara", "Katsina", "Benue")
```

### Ten of the twelve deadliest months are Borno

The exception is the point of the chart. Niger, March 2022: 491 deaths from 28 events (data), 17.5 per event, against Borno's 5 to 12. One LGA, Mariga, took 302 of them in five events (data).

Across states, events and deaths rank together at 0.86 (data). Events and deaths per event rank at 0.39 (data). Busy and lethal are not the same list.

### Deaths have a season; events do not

Events by calendar month are flat: 288 to 320 a month, on average, across equal years (data). Deaths are not. January to June average 891 deaths a month-year; July to October, 700 (data). Ratio 1.27, on the same years.

Both series are indexed to their own mean, so the shapes compare directly (proof).

```{r}
#| label: fig-season
#| echo: false
#| fig-cap: "Deaths rise in the first half; events are a flat line."

sv <- seas_m
sv$deaths_i <- 100 * sv$deaths_pm / mean(sv$deaths_pm)
sv$events_i <- 100 * sv$events_pm / mean(sv$events_pm)

fig4 <- plot_ly(data = sv, x = ~m, y = ~deaths_i, type = "bar",
  height = 400, name = "deaths", marker = list(color = pal[["deaths"]], opacity = 0.75),
  hovertemplate = "%{x}: %{y:.0f}<extra>deaths</extra>")
fig4 <- add_trace(fig4, x = ~m, y = ~events_i, type = "scatter", mode = "lines+markers",
  name = "events", line = list(color = pal[["events"]], width = 2.5),
  marker = list(color = pal[["events"]], size = 8),
  hovertemplate = "%{x}: %{y:.0f}<extra>events</extra>")
fig4 <- layout(fig4, barmode = "overlay",
  margin = list(l = 10, r = 30, t = 30, b = 10),
  legend = list(orientation = "h", x = 0, y = 1.15, font = list(size = 12)),
  shapes = list(list(type = "line", xref = "paper", x0 = 0, x1 = 1,
    y0 = 100, y1 = 100, line = list(color = pal[["ink"]], width = 1, dash = "dot"))),
  annotations = list(
    list(xref = "paper", x = 0.99, y = 1.13, showarrow = FALSE,

    list(xref = "paper", x = 0.99, y = 1.02, showarrow = FALSE,
      xanchor = "right", text = sprintf("events: %.2f to 1", ev_ratio),
      font = list(size = 12, color = pal[["events"]]))),
  xaxis = list(title = "", tickmode = "array", tickvals = 1:12,
```
