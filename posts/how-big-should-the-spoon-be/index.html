How big should the spoon be?

A correctly drawn five hundred beats a biased forty thousand. In a simulated country where the true result is fixed before anyone is interviewed, 500 interviews drawn from the whole register name the winner in 99.2% of runs and 40,000 drawn only from phone owners name it in none of them. Nasir El-Rufai dismissed Nigeria’s 2023 polls for the wrong reason, and had the right one in his next sentence.

A poll interviews two thousand people and reports a number for a country of ninety million. The objection writes itself: two thousand is a rounding error against ninety million, so how can it say anything?

Accuracy comes from the absolute size of the sample, not from the fraction of the population it covers. For a proportion \(p\) estimated from \(n\) people drawn at random,

\[SE = \sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n}}\]

in which \(N\) does not appear. It enters once, through the finite population correction:

\[SE_{\text{fpc}} = \sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n}} \times \sqrt{\frac{N-n}{N-1}}\]

That second square root is population size’s entire contribution, and it bites only when \(n\) is a serious fraction of \(N\): \(n\) buys precision, not \(n/N\). So say Freedman, Pisani and Purves, on chance errors in sampling.

That is the half people argue about. The half that decides elections is whether the spoon reached the whole pot, which no arithmetic on a poll that already missed can settle. So this ends in an invented country, where the answer is fixed before anyone is interviewed.

The translation

The standard error describes variance around \(\mathbb{E}[\hat p]\), not the distance from \(\hat p\) to \(p\). If the frame excludes part of the population then \(\mathbb{E}[\hat p] \neq p\), and that gap is unaffected by \(n\).

A cook tasting soup needs no bigger spoon for a bigger pot, so long as the pot is stirred. Stirring is the assumption, and the one that breaks. If the spoon only reaches the top, a bigger spoon reads the top more precisely and never touches the bottom. The comparison is George Gallup’s.

The claim

On 4 February 2023, three weeks before Nigeria voted, Nasir El-Rufai appeared on TVC’s Journalists’ Hangout and dismissed the opinion polls that showed Peter Obi ahead:

Opinion polls are distorted… When you have about a 100 million registered voters and you are sampling 2,000 registered voters or 300 registered voters, your results from day one are unreliable; they will have high margins of error.

He was right that the polls were wrong. Obi finished third. He said two other things in the same appearance, one about method:

Most of these polls, like the one by Bloomberg, ANAP and the one by Okonjo Iweala, all used telephone. What percentage of registered voters have phone numbers in INEC’s records? We have found out and it’s 31 per cent.

and one about his own numbers:

We have done a poll with a sample of nearly 40,000 across Nigeria and the result of the poll shows that we are ahead.

The poll he named is real and published, which makes it usable here. ANAP Foundation and NOIPolls interviewed two thousand registered voters by mobile phone in early February 2023, publishing the sample size and method with the numbers.

What the objection is worth

The objection was about the ratio of two thousand to a hundred million. That ratio has exactly one place to enter the arithmetic, so it can be priced.

Code
fpc     <- sqrt((registered - anap_n) / (registered - 1))
se_anap <- sqrt(poll_obi * (1 - poll_obi) / anap_n)

c(correction       = sprintf("%.7f", fpc),
  se_ignoring_pop  = sprintf("%.4f pp", 100 * se_anap),
  se_including_pop = sprintf("%.4f pp", 100 * se_anap * fpc),
  shrinkage        = sprintf("%.4f%%", 100 * (1 - fpc)))
      correction  se_ignoring_pop se_including_pop        shrinkage 
     "0.9999893"      "1.1117 pp"      "1.1117 pp"        "0.0011%"

The correction is 0.9999893. Knowing the population is 93,469,008 rather than unlimited improves the poll’s standard error by 0.0011% of itself. That is the whole effect of population size here, and the entire content of the objection.

The size of the miss

The margin of error says how far the estimate would wander if you ran the poll again. The miss is how far it was from the answer. One in units of the other says whether sample size is a plausible explanation.

Code
# The sample size at which the margin of error would have been as wide as the
# miss, i.e. the only n for which the objection lands.
n_cross <- ceiling(z^2 * poll_obi * (1 - poll_obi) / miss^2)

c(miss          = sprintf("%.2f pp", 100 * miss),
  miss_in_se    = sprintf("%.1f", miss / se_anap),
  n_for_margin  = comma(n_cross),
  times_smaller = sprintf("%.0f", anap_n / n_cross))
         miss    miss_in_se  n_for_margin times_smaller 
   "19.29 pp"        "17.3"          "26"          "77"

The miss was 17.3 standard errors. Sampling noise does not produce that: three is already a one-in-a-few-hundred event, and this is nearly six times further out.

Solve the margin of error backwards for the sample size that would cover it and you get 26 people, 77 times smaller than the poll run. For “your sample was too small” to explain it, the poll would have had to be a group chat. The complaint points the opposite way to the error.

Which is the point about his second sentence. The 31 per cent is about the frame, not the sample, and a frame missing two thirds of the register is the one objection on the table that no increase in \(n\) can answer. He had the correct argument, and it went unquoted.

A country where we know the answer

Everything above works backwards, pricing the objection against a miss after the fact. It cannot check whether the method finds the right answer, because in the real case the result was the only truth available and it arrived three weeks late.

So here is a country where the truth is fixed first. NaijaSim has ninety million registered voters, six zones of two states each, and four parties named after colours so that nothing reads as a claim about a person. The zone names are real and nothing else is. One thing is borrowed, the one the argument turns on: phone coverage is his 31%.

# exactly the 31 per cent he quoted. The poorest zones reach fewest.

                 `North Central` = 0.300, `South West` = 0.500,
                 `South East` = 0.440, `South South` = NA)[sim_zone]

# internally uniform. B moves 1.5x as far as A, preserving the zone average; the
# 0.5 cap keeps every state inside (0, 1).
sim_st <- NULL; sim_reg_s <- numeric(0); sim_szone <- character(0)
for (zn in sim_zone) {
  g <- sim_zs[zn, "Green"]; b <- sim_zs[zn, "Blue"]
  d <- 0.5 * min(g / 1.5, b)
  s_a <- sim_zs[zn, ]; s_a["Green"] <- g + d;       s_a["Blue"] <- b - d
  s_b <- sim_zs[zn, ]; s_b["Green"] <- g - 1.5 * d; s_b["Blue"] <- b + 1.5 * d
  sim_st    <- rbind(sim_st, s_a, s_b)
  sim_reg_s <- c(sim_reg_s, 0.60 * sim_reg_z[[zn]], 0.40 * sim_reg_z[[zn]])
  sim_szone <- c(sim_szone, zn, zn)
}
sim_frame_s <- sim_reg_s * unname(sim_reach_z[sim_szone])

# A mistyped country should fail the render rather than publish quietly.
stopifnot(

  all(abs(rowSums(sim_st) - 1) < 1e-12),

  isTRUE(all.equal(colSums(sim_st * sim_reg_s) / sum(sim_reg_s), sim_target)),

sim_ptruth  <- colSums(sim_st * sim_w_frame)
sim_frame_N <- sum(sim_frame_s)

The second column is the same country seen through a telephone. Reach is not uniform across zones and the zones do not vote alike, so the 27,900,000 voters who can be called are not a small copy of the 90,000,000 who cannot: among them Green leads by 3.54 points. Phone owners within a state vote like their neighbours here, so all of that gap is geography.

Now run the poll: stratified by state, four thousand repetitions at each setting, the register sampled at six sizes down to the 26 the inversion produced and the phone frame at the two that were quoted.

Code
# Seeded here, not left to the chunk above, so these numbers do not depend on
# how many draws the country construction happened to consume.
set.seed(20260817)

# Largest remainder: proportional shares rounded the obvious way do not sum to n.
sim_alloc <- function(n, w) {
  raw <- n * w; a <- floor(raw); k <- n - sum(a)
  if (k > 0) { j <- order(raw - a, decreasing = TRUE)[seq_len(k)]; a[j] <- a[j] + 1 }
  stopifnot(sum(a) == n)
  a
}

SIM_REPS <- 4000

# One rmultinom call per state, vectorised over all 4,000 repetitions.
sim_run <- function(n, w) {
  a <- sim_alloc(n, w)
  est <- matrix(0, SIM_REPS, 4, dimnames = list(NULL, sim_party))
  for (i in seq_along(a)) {
    if (a[i] == 0) next
    est <- est + w[i] * t(rmultinom(SIM_REPS, a[i], sim_st[i, ])) / a[i]
  }
  est
}

# "Right" means Blue tops the poll, not merely Blue ahead of Green.
sim_summ <- function(est) {
  g <- 100 * est[, "Green"]
  c(green = mean(g), sd = sd(g),
    right = 100 * mean(sim_party[max.col(est, ties.method = "first")] == "Blue"))
}

# 100.0% is also what 99.95% prints as, so the prose quotes counts, derived here.
sim_wrong <- function(pct_right) round(SIM_REPS * (100 - pct_right) / 100)

sim_ns_fair  <- c(n_cross, 100, 250, 500, 1000, anap_n)
sim_ns_phone <- c(anap_n, elrufai_n)
sim_fair  <- lapply(sim_ns_fair,  sim_run, w = sim_w_true)
sim_phone <- lapply(sim_ns_phone, sim_run, w = sim_w_frame)
sim_ft    <- t(sapply(sim_fair,  sim_summ))
sim_pt    <- t(sapply(sim_phone, sim_summ))

# Indexed by value, not position, so the prose survives a reordered ladder.
i_win <- match(500, sim_ns_fair)     # the rung the argument turns on
i_2k  <- match(anap_n, sim_ns_fair)
j_2k  <- match(anap_n, sim_ns_phone)
j_40k <- match(elrufai_n, sim_ns_phone)

data.frame(
  drawn_from   = rep(c("whole register", "phone frame"),
                     c(length(sim_ns_fair), length(sim_ns_phone))),
  interviews   = comma(c(sim_ns_fair, sim_ns_phone)),

Read the last column.

500 interviews drawn from the whole register name the winner in 3,967 of 4,000 runs. 40,000 drawn from the phone frame name it in 0. The winning poll is 80 times smaller, and wins on where its sample came from, not how big it was.

The ladder fills in the shape. Even 26 interviews, the group chat from two sections ago, get the winner right 64.1% of the time. The phone frame never climbs: 2,000 interviews pick the wrong winner 3,941 times out of 4,000, and twenty times the interviewing leaves that at 4,000. Meanwhile the wobble column does what the formula at the top promises: the scatter falls with the square root of the sample size. Every bit of precision the phone frame buys is spent stating the wrong answer more firmly.

The shape of the failure is the point. “Unreliable” is normally taken to mean wide. The phone poll at 2,000 scatters 0.96 of a point against the fair poll’s 0.87 at the same sample size, and the 40,000 curve is the narrowest on the chart. Width is not what is wrong with them: they are in the wrong place, and the arithmetic that governs width says nothing about position.

Dem ask 500 people and dem sabi? The other one ask 40,000 o.

Yes, and the five hundred got it right, the forty thousand wrong, every time. Forty thousand is a bigger spoon, not a longer one: if it never reaches past the top of the pot, the extra size only buys the wrong answer to more decimal places.

One aside, because it cuts against the original complaint from a second direction. The observed scatter at 2,000 interviews is 0.87 of a point, against the simple-random formula’s 1.02. Stratifying by state removes the between-state variation, so the poll’s method is slightly more precise than the textbook number it was attacked with.

NoteWhere the numbers come from

Quoted, not derived. The El-Rufai quotations are as reported by The Guardian Nigeria from Journalists’ Hangout on TVC News, 4 February 2023. The sample size, phone-based stratified random method and the six percentages come from ANAP Foundation’s press release; the register, votes and candidate totals are INEC’s, via the published breakdown. Outlets differ on the second figure in that first quotation, The Guardian having “300” where The Cable reports “three thousand”; the arithmetic uses ANAP’s published 2,000, so nothing turns on it. His own 40,000-sample poll has no published methodology I could find, and appears here as a sample size and nothing else.

Attribution, split deliberately. That accuracy depends on absolute sample size rather than on the sampling fraction is standard, and Freedman, Pisani and Purves develop it in the chapter on chance errors in sampling in Statistics. The soup-and-spoon comparison is attributed to George Gallup, not to them, because I could not verify that the book uses the soup wording.

NaijaSim is invented, and deliberately not a model of Nigeria. Its zone names are real; its register, zone splits, parties and result are made up. Verifiable zone-level figures for 2023 from a single authoritative source do not exist, and inventing them while calling them the past would be worse than inventing a country and saying so. Only its coverage figure of 31% and three sample sizes come from the real case: 2,000, 40,000, and the 26 the inversion produced. It is seeded, so the figures reproduce exactly.

What this does not show

Four things are doing real work, and several of them cut against the post.

The poll was wrong. Nothing above defends its finding. It defends its sample size, which is a different object. A poll can be built correctly at the scale it claims and still land 17.3 standard errors from the answer, and this one did.

The 53% who did not answer are carrying the argument. Rebasing onto decided respondents assumes the silent half would have split like the vocal half. That is the largest assumption on this page, and it is plainly false. A different assumption moves 44.68% a long way.

The 31 per cent is his figure, not a verified one. It is quoted as his claim about INEC’s records and used only to size an illustration. If true coverage is higher the frame problem is smaller than the simulation implies, though it would have to be very much higher to stop being the better objection.

NaijaSim’s coverage gap was set to a size that flips the winner. That is the honest limit of it. Different reach figures produce a phone frame that gets the answer right, and I chose ones that do not. It shows a coverage gap of the size he described is sufficient to produce a confident wrong answer that more interviewing cannot fix, not that this happened to the ANAP poll. It also assumes phone owners within a state vote like their neighbours, when real bias runs by age and income too; that everyone answers, and answers honestly, where the real poll had 53% who did neither; and that nobody changes their mind.

What survives all four is the shape. Population size entered this poll’s arithmetic once, through a term worth 0.0011%, and the error was 17.3 standard errors. Whatever went wrong here, the number of people interviewed was not it.

```{r}
#| label: correction
fpc     <- sqrt((registered - anap_n) / (registered - 1))
se_anap <- sqrt(poll_obi * (1 - poll_obi) / anap_n)

c(correction       = sprintf("%.7f", fpc),
  se_ignoring_pop  = sprintf("%.4f pp", 100 * se_anap),
  se_including_pop = sprintf("%.4f pp", 100 * se_anap * fpc),
  shrinkage        = sprintf("%.4f%%", 100 * (1 - fpc)))
```

The correction is `r sprintf("%.7f", fpc)`. Knowing the population is
`r comma(registered)` rather than unlimited improves the poll's standard error by
`r sprintf("%.4f%%", 100 * (1 - fpc))` of itself. That is the whole effect of
population size here, and the entire content of the objection.

## The size of the miss

The margin of error says how far the estimate would wander if you ran the poll
again. The miss is how far it was from the answer. One in units of the other says
whether sample size is a plausible explanation.

```{r}
#| label: distance
# The sample size at which the margin of error would have been as wide as the
# miss, i.e. the only n for which the objection lands.
n_cross <- ceiling(z^2 * poll_obi * (1 - poll_obi) / miss^2)

c(miss          = sprintf("%.2f pp", 100 * miss),
  miss_in_se    = sprintf("%.1f", miss / se_anap),
  n_for_margin  = comma(n_cross),
  times_smaller = sprintf("%.0f", anap_n / n_cross))
```

The miss was `r sprintf("%.1f", miss / se_anap)` standard errors. Sampling noise
does not produce that: three is already a one-in-a-few-hundred event, and this is
nearly six times further out.

Solve the margin of error backwards for the sample size that would cover it and
you get `r comma(n_cross)` people, `r sprintf("%.0f", anap_n / n_cross)` times
*smaller* than the poll run. For "your sample was too small" to explain it, the
poll would have had to be a group chat. The complaint points the opposite way to
the error.

Which is the point about his second sentence. The 31 per cent is about the frame,
not the sample, and a frame missing two thirds of the register is the one
objection on the table that no increase in $n$ can answer. He had the correct
argument, and it went unquoted.

## A country where we know the answer

Everything above works backwards, pricing the objection against a miss after the
fact. It cannot check whether the method *finds* the right answer, because in
the real case the result was the only truth available and it arrived three weeks
late.

So here is a country where the truth is fixed first. NaijaSim has ninety million
registered voters, six zones of two states each, and four parties named after
colours so that nothing reads as a claim about a person. The zone names are real
and nothing else is. One thing is borrowed, the one the argument turns on: phone
coverage is his `r sprintf("%.0f%%", 100 * phone_share)`.

```{r}
#| label: naijasim
#| message: false
set.seed(20260817)

sim_party <- c("Green", "Blue", "Red", "Yellow")
sim_zone  <- c("North West", "North East", "North Central",
               "South West", "South East", "South South")
sim_reg_z <- c(21, 12, 15, 18, 9, 15) * 1e6      # registered voters, by zone
names(sim_reg_z) <- sim_zone

# The national result, set by fiat. Blue wins.
sim_target <- c(Green = 0.30, Blue = 0.38, Red = 0.24, Yellow = 0.08)

# Zone shares are chosen, EXCEPT North Central, solved so the register-weighted
# truth lands exactly on sim_target.
sim_zs <- rbind(
  c(0.08, 0.50, 0.36, 0.06),
  c(0.10, 0.52, 0.32, 0.06),
  c(NA,   NA,   NA,   NA  ),
  c(0.34, 0.34, 0.22, 0.10),
  c(0.72, 0.12, 0.08, 0.08),
  c(0.48, 0.26, 0.16, 0.10)
)
dimnames(sim_zs) <- list(sim_zone, sim_party)
i_nc <- match("North Central", sim_zone)
sim_zs[i_nc, ] <- (sum(sim_reg_z) * sim_target -
                   colSums(sim_zs[-i_nc, ] * sim_reg_z[-i_nc])) / sim_reg_z[i_nc]

# Phone reach is chosen, EXCEPT South South, solved so national coverage is
# exactly the 31 per cent he quoted. The poorest zones reach fewest.
sim_reach_z <- c(`North West` = 0.160, `North East` = 0.120,
                 `North Central` = 0.300, `South West` = 0.500,
                 `South East` = 0.440, `South South` = NA)[sim_zone]
i_ss <- match("South South", sim_zone)
sim_reach_z[i_ss] <- (phone_share * sum(sim_reg_z) -
                      sum((sim_reg_z * sim_reach_z)[-i_ss])) / sim_reg_z[i_ss]

# Each zone splits 60/40 into two states leaning opposite ways, so no stratum is
# internally uniform. B moves 1.5x as far as A, preserving the zone average; the
# 0.5 cap keeps every state inside (0, 1).
sim_st <- NULL; sim_reg_s <- numeric(0); sim_szone <- character(0)
for (zn in sim_zone) {
  g <- sim_zs[zn, "Green"]; b <- sim_zs[zn, "Blue"]
  d <- 0.5 * min(g / 1.5, b)
  s_a <- sim_zs[zn, ]; s_a["Green"] <- g + d;       s_a["Blue"] <- b - d
  s_b <- sim_zs[zn, ]; s_b["Green"] <- g - 1.5 * d; s_b["Blue"] <- b + 1.5 * d
  sim_st    <- rbind(sim_st, s_a, s_b)
  sim_reg_s <- c(sim_reg_s, 0.60 * sim_reg_z[[zn]], 0.40 * sim_reg_z[[zn]])
  sim_szone <- c(sim_szone, zn, zn)
}
sim_frame_s <- sim_reg_s * unname(sim_reach_z[sim_szone])

# A mistyped country should fail the render rather than publish quietly.
stopifnot(
  sum(sim_reg_z) == 90e6,
  all(abs(rowSums(sim_st) - 1) < 1e-12),
  all(sim_st > 0), all(sim_st < 1),
  isTRUE(all.equal(colSums(sim_st * sim_reg_s) / sum(sim_reg_s), sim_target)),
  isTRUE(all.equal(sum(sim_frame_s) / sum(sim_reg_s), phone_share))
)

sim_w_true  <- sim_reg_s   / sum(sim_reg_s)     # a fair poll's weights
sim_w_frame <- sim_frame_s / sum(sim_frame_s)   # what a phone poll actually gets
sim_truth   <- colSums(sim_st * sim_w_true)
sim_ptruth  <- colSums(sim_st * sim_w_frame)
sim_frame_N <- sum(sim_frame_s)

data.frame(
  party            = sim_party,
  whole_register   = sprintf("%.2f%%", 100 * sim_truth),
  phone_frame_only = sprintf("%.2f%%", 100 * sim_ptruth)
)
```

Blue wins NaijaSim by
`r sprintf("%.0f", 100 * (sim_truth[["Blue"]] - sim_truth[["Green"]]))` points,
the fact a poll is being asked to find.

The second column is the same country seen through a telephone. Reach is not
uniform across zones and the zones do not vote alike, so the
`r comma(sim_frame_N)` voters who can be called are not a small copy of the
`r comma(sum(sim_reg_z))` who cannot: among them Green leads by
`r sprintf("%.2f", 100 * (sim_ptruth[["Green"]] - sim_ptruth[["Blue"]]))` points.
Phone owners within a state vote like their neighbours here, so all of that gap
is geography.

Now run the poll: stratified by state, four thousand repetitions at each setting,
the register sampled at six sizes down to the `r comma(n_cross)` the inversion
produced and the phone frame at the two that were quoted.

```{r}
#| label: naijasim-runs
# Seeded here, not left to the chunk above, so these numbers do not depend on
# how many draws the country construction happened to consume.
set.seed(20260817)

# Largest remainder: proportional shares rounded the obvious way do not sum to n.
sim_alloc <- function(n, w) {
  raw <- n * w; a <- floor(raw); k <- n - sum(a)
  if (k > 0) { j <- order(raw - a, decreasing = TRUE)[seq_len(k)]; a[j] <- a[j] + 1 }
  stopifnot(sum(a) == n)
  a
}

SIM_REPS <- 4000

# One rmultinom call per state, vectorised over all 4,000 repetitions.
sim_run <- function(n, w) {
  a <- sim_alloc(n, w)
  est <- matrix(0, SIM_REPS, 4, dimnames = list(NULL, sim_party))
  for (i in seq_along(a)) {
    if (a[i] == 0) next
    est <- est + w[i] * t(rmultinom(SIM_REPS, a[i], sim_st[i, ])) / a[i]
  }
  est
}

# "Right" means Blue tops the poll, not merely Blue ahead of Green.
sim_summ <- function(est) {
  g <- 100 * est[, "Green"]
  c(green = mean(g), sd = sd(g),
    right = 100 * mean(sim_party[max.col(est, ties.method = "first")] == "Blue"))
}

# 100.0% is also what 99.95% prints as, so the prose quotes counts, derived here.
sim_wrong <- function(pct_right) round(SIM_REPS * (100 - pct_right) / 100)

sim_ns_fair  <- c(n_cross, 100, 250, 500, 1000, anap_n)
sim_ns_phone <- c(anap_n, elrufai_n)
sim_fair  <- lapply(sim_ns_fair,  sim_run, w = sim_w_true)
sim_phone <- lapply(sim_ns_phone, sim_run, w = sim_w_frame)
sim_ft    <- t(sapply(sim_fair,  sim_summ))
sim_pt    <- t(sapply(sim_phone, sim_summ))

# Indexed by value, not position, so the prose survives a reordered ladder.
i_win <- match(500, sim_ns_fair)     # the rung the argument turns on
i_2k  <- match(anap_n, sim_ns_fair)
j_2k  <- match(anap_n, sim_ns_phone)
j_40k <- match(elrufai_n, sim_ns_phone)

data.frame(
  drawn_from   = rep(c("whole register", "phone frame"),
                     c(length(sim_ns_fair), length(sim_ns_phone))),
  interviews   = comma(c(sim_ns_fair, sim_ns_phone)),
  green_says   = sprintf("%.2f%%", c(sim_ft[, "green"], sim_pt[, "green"])),
  wobble       = sprintf("%.2f pp", c(sim_ft[, "sd"], sim_pt[, "sd"])),
  winner_right = sprintf("%.1f%%", c(sim_ft[, "right"], sim_pt[, "right"]))
)
```

Read the last column.

`r comma(sim_ns_fair[i_win])` interviews drawn from the whole register name the
winner in `r comma(SIM_REPS - sim_wrong(sim_ft[i_win, "right"]))` of
`r comma(SIM_REPS)` runs. `r comma(elrufai_n)` drawn from the phone frame name
it in `r comma(SIM_REPS - sim_wrong(sim_pt[j_40k, "right"]))`. The winning poll
is `r sprintf("%.0f", elrufai_n / sim_ns_fair[i_win])` times smaller, and wins on
where its sample came from, not how big it was.

The ladder fills in the shape. Even `r comma(n_cross)` interviews, the group
chat from two sections ago, get the winner right
`r sprintf("%.1f%%", sim_ft[1, "right"])` of the time. The phone frame never
climbs: `r comma(anap_n)` interviews pick the wrong winner
`r comma(sim_wrong(sim_pt[j_2k, "right"]))` times out of `r comma(SIM_REPS)`, and
twenty times the interviewing leaves that at
`r comma(sim_wrong(sim_pt[j_40k, "right"]))`. Meanwhile the wobble column does
what the formula at the top promises: the scatter falls with the square root of
the sample size. Every bit of precision the phone frame buys is spent stating the
wrong answer more firmly.

## Where the numbers come from

**Quoted, not derived.** The El-Rufai quotations are as reported by
[The Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/news/el-rufai-debunks-obis-election-win-predictions-calls-opinion-polls-a-joke/)
from *Journalists' Hangout* on TVC News, 4 February 2023. The sample size,
phone-based stratified random method and the six percentages come from
[ANAP Foundation's press release](https://www.anapfoundation.com/press-releases/2023-elections-peter-obi-maintains-lead-amongst-presidential-poll-respondents);
the register, votes and candidate totals are INEC's, via the
[published breakdown](https://dailypost.ng/2023/03/01/2023-elections-breakdown-of-results-as-tinubu-defeats-atiku-obi/).
Outlets differ on the second figure in that first quotation, The Guardian having
"300" where
[The Cable](https://www.thecable.ng/noi-poll-weve-been-using-same-methodology-since-2015-anap-foundation-replies-critics/)
reports "three thousand"; the arithmetic uses ANAP's published
`r comma(anap_n)`, so nothing turns on it. His own `r comma(elrufai_n)`-sample
poll has no published methodology I could find, and appears here as a sample size
and nothing else.

**Attribution, split deliberately.** That accuracy depends on absolute sample
size rather than on the sampling fraction is standard, and Freedman, Pisani and
Purves develop it in the chapter on chance errors in sampling in *Statistics*.
The soup-and-spoon comparison is attributed to
[George Gallup](https://nrich.maths.org/13895), not to them, because I could not
verify that the book uses the soup wording.

**NaijaSim is invented, and deliberately not a model of Nigeria.** Its zone names
are real; its register, zone splits, parties and result are made up. Verifiable
zone-level figures for 2023 from a single authoritative source do not exist, and
inventing them while calling them the past would be worse than inventing a
country and saying so. Only its coverage figure of
`r sprintf("%.0f%%", 100 * phone_share)` and three sample sizes come from the
real case: `r comma(anap_n)`, `r comma(elrufai_n)`, and the `r comma(n_cross)`
the inversion produced. It is seeded, so the figures reproduce exactly.
:::

## What this does not show

Four things are doing real work, and several of them cut against the post.

**The poll was wrong.** Nothing above defends its finding. It defends its sample
size, which is a different object. A poll can be built correctly at the scale it
claims and still land `r sprintf("%.1f", miss / se_anap)` standard errors from
the answer, and this one did.

**The `r sprintf("%.0f%%", poll[["Undecided"]] + poll[["Refused"]])` who did not
answer are carrying the argument.** Rebasing onto decided respondents assumes the
silent half would have split like the vocal half. That is the largest assumption
on this page, and it is plainly false. A different assumption moves
`r sprintf("%.2f%%", 100 * poll_obi)` a long way.

**The 31 per cent is his figure, not a verified one.** It is quoted as his claim
about INEC's records and used only to size an illustration. If true coverage is
higher the frame problem is smaller than the simulation implies, though it would
have to be very much higher to stop being the better objection.

**NaijaSim's coverage gap was set to a size that flips the winner.** That is the
honest limit of it. Different reach figures produce a phone frame that gets the
answer right, and I chose ones that do not. It shows a coverage gap of the size
he described is *sufficient* to produce a confident wrong answer that more
interviewing cannot fix, not that this happened to the ANAP poll. It also assumes
phone owners within a state vote like their neighbours, when real bias runs by
age and income too; that everyone answers, and answers honestly, where the real
poll had `r sprintf("%.0f%%", poll[["Undecided"]] + poll[["Refused"]])` who did
neither; and that nobody changes their mind.

What survives all four is the shape. Population size entered this poll's
arithmetic once, through a term worth
`r sprintf("%.4f%%", 100 * (1 - fpc))`, and the error was
`r sprintf("%.1f", miss / se_anap)` standard errors. Whatever went wrong here,
the number of people interviewed was not it.
