Question

Polls put Peter Obi first; he finished third. At 2,000 interviews the margin is ±2.18 points (proof). The miss was 19.29 points (proof). Was the sample too small?

Claim

On TVC, three weeks before the vote, El-Rufai dismissed the polls:

Opinion polls are distorted… When you have about a 100 million registered voters and you are sampling 2,000 registered voters or 300 registered voters, your results from day one are unreliable; they will have high margins of error.

He was right that they were wrong.

Counter-claim

His next sentence is the better objection.

Most of these polls, like the one by Bloomberg, ANAP and the one by Okonjo Iweala, all used telephone. What percentage of registered voters have phone numbers in INEC’s records? We have found out and it’s 31 per cent.

Only 31 per cent of the register can be telephoned (assume). The simulation prices it. Five hundred, whole register: winner right 3,967 of 4,000 (sim). Forty thousand, phones: winner right 0 of 4,000 (sim).

Setup

NaijaSim is invented: 90,000,000 voters, six zones, four colour-named parties (assume). Phone reach is his 31 per cent (assume). The truth is fixed before anyone is interviewed.

# Largest remainder: proportional shares rounded the obvious way do not sum to n.
sim_alloc <- function(n, w) {
  raw <- n * w; a <- floor(raw); k <- n - sum(a)
  if (k > 0) { j <- order(raw - a, decreasing = TRUE)[seq_len(k)]; a[j] <- a[j] + 1 }
  stopifnot(sum(a) == n)
  a
}

SIM_REPS <- 4000

# One rmultinom call per state, vectorised over all 4,000 repetitions.
sim_run <- function(n, w) {
  a <- sim_alloc(n, w)
  est <- matrix(0, SIM_REPS, 4, dimnames = list(NULL, sim_party))
  for (i in seq_along(a)) {
    if (a[i] == 0) next
    est <- est + w[i] * t(rmultinom(SIM_REPS, a[i], sim_st[i, ])) / a[i]
  }
  est
}

# "Right" means Blue tops the poll, not merely Blue ahead of Green.
sim_summ <- function(est) {
  g <- 100 * est[, "Green"]
  c(green = mean(g), sd = sd(g),
    right = 100 * mean(sim_party[max.col(est, ties.method = "first")] == "Blue"))
}

What a phone frame actually is

Through a phone, Green leads by 3.5 points (sim). In the country, Blue leads by 8 (sim). The figure is not a smaller copy of the country; it is a different country, one that leans Green. Each coloured box is one party’s share. The two groups are the two frames. They are the invented country’s truth, not Nigeria’s result.

Pattern: each bar is a party, coloured by its name; the phone frame lifts Green and cuts Blue, while the dotted lines mark the country’s true shares.

The spoon, priced: population size buys nothing

Population size is worth 0.0011% of the margin (proof). That is his objection, priced.

Pattern: the two lines are one line; the shaded sliver is the entire population correction.

Four thousand runs: sample size is not the fix

Five hundred beats forty thousand, eighty times smaller (sim). The frame never climbs. The ladder: Blue tops 4,000 polls 64% of the time at 500 interviews and 100% at 2,000; never, even at 40,000, on the phone frame. The trend is precision, not accuracy. More interviews sharpen a fair poll toward 100 (sim). The phone line never leaves zero (sim).

Pattern: one line climbs, the other flatlines at zero — the gap is the frame.

The same answer, harder: precision without accuracy

At 2,000, phones wobble 0.96 points against 0.87 fair (sim). Narrowest, and furthest from the truth.

Pattern: four bell curves, narrowest on the right; the truth lines mark each frame’s centre.

NoteWhere the numbers come from

The margin. For a proportion \(p\) from \(n\) draws, \(SE=\sqrt{p(1-p)/n}\). Population size enters only through the finite population correction \(\sqrt{(N-n)/(N-1)}\), which at \(n=2{,}000\) against \(N=93{,}469{,}008\) is 0.9999893 — a shrinkage of 0.0011% of the standard error.

The miss. Obi polled 44.68% of decided respondents and took 25.40% of valid votes: a gap of 19.29 points, or 17.3 standard errors. Inverting the margin for the sample size that would cover a miss that large gives 26 interviews — the rung on the left of the ladder, and the reason the ladder starts there.

The country. NaijaSim is invented and deliberately not a model of Nigeria. Zone names are real; register, zone splits, parties and result are made up, because verifiable zone-level 2023 figures do not exist from one authoritative source and inventing them while calling them the past would be worse than inventing a country and saying so. Ninety million registered, six zones, four colour-named parties, and coverage of 31% borrowed from his own figure. Seeded at 20260817; 4,000 repetitions at each sample size; stratified by state.

Quoted, not derived. The El-Rufai quotations are as reported by The Guardian Nigeria from Journalists’ Hangout on TVC News, 4 February 2023. The sample size, phone-based stratified random method and the six percentages come from ANAP Foundation’s press release; the register and valid votes are INEC’s, via the published breakdown. Outlets differ on the second figure in that first quotation, The Guardian having “300” where The Cable reports “three thousand”; the arithmetic uses ANAP’s published 2,000, so nothing turns on it. His own 40,000-sample poll has no published methodology I could find, and appears here as a sample size and nothing else.

The portrait. File:Nasir Ahmed el-Rufai 2023.jpg on Wikimedia Commons, 470 × 662, tagged PD-VOA: a Voice of America work, so public domain in the United States rather than openly licensed. The Commons page records it as a VOA Africa upload, which is what makes the tag stick.

Attribution, split deliberately. That accuracy depends on absolute sample size rather than on the sampling fraction is standard, and Freedman, Pisani and Purves develop it in the chapter on chance errors in sampling in Statistics. The soup-and-spoon comparison is attributed to George Gallup, not to them, because I could not verify that the book uses the soup wording.

So five hundred people told you who won, and forty thousand did not?

He was half right. The phone polls were wrong, and the simulation agrees every run. But sample size was not why. Forty thousand phone interviews named the winner 0 times in 4,000 (sim). Five hundred from the whole register named it 3,967 times (sim). The spoon was never too small. It never reached the bottom.

The formulas, said twice

The post runs on two pieces of arithmetic: his objection, and the simulation that prices it. Here they are both ways.

His objection, as arithmetic

\[\mathrm{SE}(\hat p)=\sqrt{\frac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{n}},\qquad \mathrm{MOE}=z_{0.975}\,\mathrm{SE}\]

The margin depends on \(n\), the number asked — not on \(N\), the number who exist. At 2,000 interviews it is ±2.18 points; at 40,000, ±0.49. Neither is 19 points.

\[\mathrm{fpc}=\sqrt{\frac{N-n}{N-1}}\]

The only place the size of the register enters at all. At 2,000 against 93,469,008 it shrinks the margin by 0.0011 per cent — which is why the spoon, not the soup, is what he was describing.

\[n^{\star}=\frac{z^{2}\,\hat p(1-\hat p)}{\text{miss}^{2}}\]

The sample that would have made his sentence true: 26 interviews, against an actual miss of 19.29 points. A poll small enough to miss by that much would be a poll of twenty-six people.

What the simulation computes

\[\hat p_j^{(b)}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbf 1\big[\text{voter }k\text{ backs }j\big]\]

One run \(b\), of 4,000. Interview \(n\) people, count who backs each party, and divide by \(n\). The frame decides who the \(n\) people are; nothing else differs between the runs.

\[\hat{\Pr}(\text{right})=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\mathbf 1\big[\text{top party in run } b \text{ is Blue}\big]\]

The number quoted in the text: the share of 4,000 runs whose top party is the true top party. 3,967 of 4,000 at 500 on the register; 0 of 4,000 at 40,000 by phone.

\[\tilde\pi_z=\frac{\pi_z\,r_z}{\sum_{z'}\pi_{z'}\,r_{z'}}\]

The phone frame, in one line. Zone \(z\) is weighted by its phone reach \(r_z\) instead of its population \(\pi_z\). That single substitution is the entire difference between the two countries in the first figure.

## Where the numbers come from

**The margin.** For a proportion $p$ from $n$ draws, $SE=\sqrt{p(1-p)/n}$. Population size enters
only through the finite population correction $\sqrt{(N-n)/(N-1)}$, which at
$n=2{,}000$ against $N=93{,}469{,}008$ is `r sprintf("%.7f", fpc)` — a shrinkage of
`r sprintf("%.4f%%", 100 * (1 - fpc))` of the standard error.

**The miss.** Obi polled `r sprintf("%.2f%%", 100 * poll_obi)` of decided respondents and took
`r sprintf("%.2f%%", 100 * act_obi)` of valid votes: a gap of `r sprintf("%.2f", 100 * miss)`
points, or `r sprintf("%.1f", miss / se_anap)` standard errors. Inverting the margin for the sample
size that would cover a miss that large gives `r comma(n_cross)` interviews — the rung on the left
of the ladder, and the reason the ladder starts there.

**The country.** NaijaSim is invented and deliberately not a model of Nigeria. Zone names are real;
register, zone splits, parties and result are made up, because verifiable zone-level 2023 figures do
not exist from one authoritative source and inventing them while calling them the past would be
worse than inventing a country and saying so. Ninety million registered, six zones, four
colour-named parties, and coverage of `r sprintf("%.0f%%", 100 * phone_share)` borrowed from his own
figure. Seeded at 20260817; 4,000 repetitions at each sample size; stratified by state.

**Quoted, not derived.** The El-Rufai quotations are as reported by
[The Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/news/el-rufai-debunks-obis-election-win-predictions-calls-opinion-polls-a-joke/)
from *Journalists' Hangout* on TVC News, 4 February 2023. The sample size, phone-based stratified
random method and the six percentages come from
[ANAP Foundation's press release](https://www.anapfoundation.com/press-releases/2023-elections-peter-obi-maintains-lead-amongst-presidential-poll-respondents);
the register and valid votes are INEC's, via the
[published breakdown](https://dailypost.ng/2023/03/01/2023-elections-breakdown-of-results-as-tinubu-defeats-atiku-obi/).
Outlets differ on the second figure in that first quotation, The Guardian having "300" where
[The Cable](https://www.thecable.ng/noi-poll-weve-been-using-same-methodology-since-2015-anap-foundation-replies-critics/)
reports "three thousand"; the arithmetic uses ANAP's published `r comma(anap_n)`, so nothing turns
on it. His own `r comma(elrufai_n)`-sample poll has no published methodology I could find, and
appears here as a sample size and nothing else.

**The portrait.** `File:Nasir Ahmed el-Rufai 2023.jpg` on Wikimedia Commons, 470 × 662, tagged
PD-VOA: a Voice of America work, so public domain in the United States rather than openly licensed.
The Commons page records it as a VOA Africa upload, which is what makes the tag stick.

**Attribution, split deliberately.** That accuracy depends on absolute sample size rather than on
the sampling fraction is standard, and Freedman, Pisani and Purves develop it in the chapter on
chance errors in sampling in *Statistics*. The soup-and-spoon comparison is attributed to
[George Gallup](https://nrich.maths.org/13895), not to them, because I could not verify that the
book uses the soup wording.
:::

## The formulas, said twice

The post runs on two pieces of arithmetic: his objection, and the simulation that prices it. Here they are both ways.

### His objection, as arithmetic

::: {.dual}
::: {.spine}
$$\mathrm{SE}(\hat p)=\sqrt{\frac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{n}},\qquad \mathrm{MOE}=z_{0.975}\,\mathrm{SE}$$
:::

::: {.dub}
The margin depends on $n$, the number asked — not on $N$, the number who exist. At 2,000 interviews it is ±2.18 points; at 40,000, ±0.49. Neither is 19 points.
:::

::: {.spine}
$$\mathrm{fpc}=\sqrt{\frac{N-n}{N-1}}$$
:::

::: {.dub}
The only place the size of the register enters at all. At 2,000 against 93,469,008 it shrinks the margin by 0.0011 per cent — which is why the spoon, not the soup, is what he was describing.
:::

::: {.spine}
$$n^{\star}=\frac{z^{2}\,\hat p(1-\hat p)}{\text{miss}^{2}}$$
:::

::: {.dub}
The sample that would have made his sentence true: 26 interviews, against an actual miss of 19.29 points. A poll small enough to miss by that much would be a poll of twenty-six people.
:::
:::

### What the simulation computes

::: {.dual}
::: {.spine}
$$\hat p_j^{(b)}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbf 1\big[\text{voter }k\text{ backs }j\big]$$
:::

::: {.dub}
One run $b$, of 4,000. Interview $n$ people, count who backs each party, and divide by $n$. The frame decides who the $n$ people are; nothing else differs between the runs.
:::

::: {.spine}
$$\hat{\Pr}(\text{right})=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\mathbf 1\big[\text{top party in run } b \text{ is Blue}\big]$$
:::

::: {.dub}
The number quoted in the text: the share of 4,000 runs whose top party is the true top party. 3,967 of 4,000 at 500 on the register; 0 of 4,000 at 40,000 by phone.
:::

::: {.spine}
$$\tilde\pi_z=\frac{\pi_z\,r_z}{\sum_{z'}\pi_{z'}\,r_{z'}}$$
:::

::: {.dub}
The phone frame, in one line. Zone $z$ is weighted by its phone reach $r_z$ instead of its population $\pi_z$. That single substitution is the entire difference between the two countries in the first figure.
:::
:::
